3. Risks of tennis, and you accept them

Tennis is a physical sport, and playing it carries real risks, some of which can't be removed without changing the game. They include, among others:

  • sprains, strains, pulled muscles, tendon and joint injuries, fractures, and overuse injuries;
  • being hit by a ball, a racket, or another player; collisions, slips, trips, and falls;
  • eye injuries, concussions, and head injuries;
  • heart problems, heat exhaustion, dehydration, sunburn, and cold;
  • wet, uneven, worn, or poorly lit surfaces; nets, posts, fences, benches, and debris;
  • the conduct of other players, spectators, and members of the public in a public park;
  • travel to and from courts and any other venue;
  • loss of, theft of, or damage to your belongings;
  • and, in rare cases, serious injury, permanent disability, or death.

You understand these risks and voluntarily accept all of them, whether or not they're listed here, for every club activity you take part in.
